\begin{abstract}
We study planar polynomial maps through the scheme of pairs with the same
image.  For
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C},
\]
put \(A=\C[x_1,x_2]\), \(S=\C[x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2]\), and let
\(I_R(F)\subseteq I_\Delta\subset S\) be the collision and diagonal ideals.
Their quotient \(\Obs(F)=I_\Delta/I_R(F)\) is the diagonal ideal in
\(C_F=S/I_R(F)\).  For a Keller map, ordered divided differences give an
explicit idempotent \(p_F\in C_F\) with \(\Obs(F)=C_Fp_F\).  Evaluating
this projector on distinct collision sheets over a domain yields a
unit-ideal certificate for their coordinate differences.

Write \(B=\C[P,Q]\subseteq A\), \(K=\Frac(B)\), \(L=\Frac(A)\), and let
\(N/K\) be the normal closure with marked embedding
\(\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow N\) and Galois group \(G\).  In the
all-conjugate algebra \(E=B[g(\iota(A)):g\in G]\subseteq N\), the
certificate makes \((\sigma(e)-e:e\in E)=E\) for every \(\sigma\ne1\).
Consequently \(E/E^G\) is finite \'etale and the action-graph map
\(E\otimes_{E^G}E\simeq\prod_{g\in G}E\) is an isomorphism.  Its base is
\(E^G\), not necessarily \(B\); this does not prove planar obstruction
vanishing.  As an application, a generic-degree-two Keller map would have
\(p_F\ne0\) from its residual generic collision factor, but \(p_F=0\)
from classical Keller--Galois rigidity.  Hence no planar Keller map has
generic degree two.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The complex two-dimensional Jacobian conjecture \(JC(2)\) asks whether every
polynomial map
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C},
  \qquad \det JF\in\C^\times,
\]
has a polynomial inverse.  Such an \(F\) is called a \emph{Keller map}.
The condition on the Jacobian is local, while polynomial invertibility is
global.  Collision geometry makes the global issue visible by retaining
pairs \((x,y)\) for which \(F(x)=F(y)\).

The companion paper \cite{VanDerVlugt2026CubicCollision} develops the
dimension-independent collision construction.  It associates to a
polynomial map two ideals
\[
  I_R(F)=\bigl(F_i(x)-F_i(y)\bigr),
  \qquad
  I_\Delta=\bigl(x_i-y_i\bigr)
\]
inside the coordinate ring of ordered pairs.  The containment
\(I_R(F)\subseteq I_\Delta\) induces the obstruction ideal
\[
  \Obs(F)=I_\Delta/I_R(F)
\]
in the collision ring \(C_F\).  This ideal defines the diagonal inside the
collision scheme.  Its vanishing says that the collision scheme consists
only of the diagonal; over \(\C\), this is equivalent to polynomial
automorphy.

The same construction has a generic field-theoretic form.  Write
\[
  A=\C[x_1,x_2],\qquad
  B=\C[P,Q]\subseteq A,\qquad
  K=\Frac(B),\qquad
  L=\Frac(A).
\]
For a Keller map, \(L/K\) is finite and separable, and
\[
  d_F=[L:K]
\]
is the generic degree of \(F\).  At the generic point, Paper~I gives the
compatible isomorphism
\[
  K\otimes_BC_F\simeq_KL\otimes_KL,
\]
with base-changed diagonal evaluation equal to tensor multiplication.

Paper~I already uses classical Galois rigidity to exclude counterexamples
of generic degrees one and two in every dimension
\cite[Corollary~4.1]{VanDerVlugt2026CubicCollision}.  Here we construct an
explicit planar collision projector and evaluate it on the conjugate
source sheets of the normal closure.  This gives a scheme-theoretic
fixed-locus certificate and a finite \(G\)-cover over the algebra of
invariants.  The quadratic exclusion is an application, not the sole
result.  Moskowicz states a broader planar prime-degree exclusion
\cite{Moskowicz2024PrimeDegree}; we do not use that argument.

The projector construction has no generic-degree restriction.  Ordered
divided differences give the planar multivariate B\'ezoutian determinant
and an idempotent \(p_F\) generating \(\Obs(F)\).  On any two distinct
collision sheets over a domain, its evaluation is \(1\), forcing their
coordinate differences to generate the unit ideal.  For the
all-conjugate algebra \(E\subseteq N\), this excludes the
scheme-theoretic fixed locus of every nonidentity element of
\(G=\Gal(N/K)\) and identifies \(E/E^G\) as a finite \(G\)-cover.  Its
base is \(E^G\), which is not identified with \(B\) here.

The application to \(d_F=2\) uses two complementary descriptions of the
collision obstruction.  At the generic point, a separable quadratic
extension gives
\[
  L\otimes_KL\simeq_L L\times L,
\]
with tensor multiplication equal to the first-coordinate map.  The second
factor forces \(\Obs(F)\neq0\), and under the Keller condition this gives
\(p_F\neq0\).

Classical Galois rigidity gives the opposite conclusion.  A separable
quadratic extension is normal, so a hypothetical planar Keller map with
\(d_F=2\) would be a polynomial automorphism
\cite{Campbell1973,Razar1979,Wright1981}.  Its obstruction and projector
would vanish.  The contradiction excludes generic degree two.

The marked normal closure gives a degree-independent vanishing criterion.
Let \(N/K\) be the normal closure of \(L/K\), with marked inclusion
\(\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow N\), and let \(H\) fix \(\iota(L)\).
For a planar Keller map, vanishing is equivalent to \(N=\iota(L)\), or
\(H=1\).  This is the normal-branch counterpart to Paper~I's nonnormal
cubic construction.

Section~\ref{sec:collision-inputs} recalls, without reproving, the general
construction needed here.  Section~\ref{sec:secant-projector} constructs
the planar projector, and Section~\ref{sec:conjugate-cover} evaluates it on
conjugate sheets.  Section~\ref{sec:quadratic-collision} proves
quadratic collision nonvanishing, and
Section~\ref{sec:rigidity-contradiction} combines it with Galois rigidity.
Section~\ref{sec:vanishing-criterion} gives the marked-closure vanishing
criterion.  After the conclusion, Section~\ref{sec:lean-status} records
the formalization.

The degree-two exclusion is not a proof of all of \(JC(2)\).  Remaining
possible counterexamples have nonnormal function-field extensions of
generic degree at least three.  The finite cover over \(E^G\) does not
show that \(E^G=B\) or eliminate the boundary over \(\Spec B\).

\section{Inputs from Paper I}
\label{sec:collision-inputs}

We use the dimension-independent construction of Paper~I
\cite{VanDerVlugt2026CubicCollision}.  To keep the two manuscripts
notationally identical, let
\[
  F=(F_1,\dots,F_n)\colon\A^n_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^n_{\C},
\]
and put
\[
  \begin{gathered}
  A=\C[x_1,\dots,x_n],\qquad
  S=\C[x_1,\dots,x_n,y_1,\dots,y_n],\\
  I_R(F)=\bigl(F_i(x)-F_i(y)\bigr)_{i=1}^n,\qquad
  I_\Delta=\bigl(x_i-y_i\bigr)_{i=1}^n,\\
  C_F=S/I_R(F),\qquad
  R_F=\Spec C_F.
  \end{gathered}
\]
Since \(I_R(F)\subseteq I_\Delta\), diagonal substitution descends along
the quotient map \(q_F\colon S\twoheadrightarrow C_F\) to
\[
  \bar\mu_F\colon C_F\twoheadrightarrow A,
  \qquad [s]\longmapsto s(x,x).
\]
The image of \(I_\Delta\) is the ideal
\[
  \Obs(F):=q_F(I_\Delta)=I_\Delta/I_R(F)\subseteq C_F,
\]
which defines the diagonal inside \(R_F\).

The following two results recall Paper~I's diagonal-kernel and automorphism
criteria \cite[Proposition~2.2 and Theorem~2.7]{VanDerVlugt2026CubicCollision}
and its generic collision bridge
\cite[Theorem~2.5]{VanDerVlugt2026CubicCollision}.  No proof of the general
collision construction is repeated here.

\begin{theorem}[Diagonal kernel and automorphism detection]
\label{thm:quoted-diagonal-kernel}
For every complex polynomial map as above,
\[
  \ker(\bar\mu_F)=\Obs(F)=I_\Delta/I_R(F).
\]
Moreover,
\[
  F\text{ is a polynomial automorphism}
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  I_R(F)=I_\Delta
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
  \Obs(F)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

The implication \(\Obs(F)=0\Rightarrow F\) is a polynomial automorphism
uses Ax--Grothendieck
\cite{Ax1969}.  The degree-two exclusion needs only the elementary
forward implication.

Let
\[
  B=\C[F_1,\dots,F_n]\subseteq A,\qquad
  K=\Frac(B),\qquad
  L=\Frac(A),
\]
and let
\[
  \mu_L\colon L\otimes_KL\longrightarrow L,
  \qquad \ell\otimes\ell'\longmapsto\ell\ell'
\]
be tensor multiplication.

\begin{theorem}[Generic collision bridge]
\label{thm:quoted-generic-bridge}
If \(L/K\) is finite, then
\[
  K\otimes_BA\simeq_KL
\]
and there is a \(K\)-algebra isomorphism
\[
  \Phi_F\colon K\otimes_BC_F\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }L\otimes_KL
\]
which carries the base-changed diagonal evaluation to \(\mu_L\).
Furthermore, flatness of the localization \(B\to K\) identifies
\[
  K\otimes_B\Obs(F)
  \simeq
  \ker(1\otimes\bar\mu_F)
\]
as \(K\)-vector spaces.
\end{theorem}

These statements combine the fiber-product, generic-base-change, and
diagonal-kernel results of Paper~I.  Their compatibility with \(\mu_L\)
marks which generic factor is the diagonal.

Return now to
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C}.
\]
For a Keller map, \(P,Q\) are algebraically independent and \(L/K\) is
finite separable.  We write
\[
  d_F:=[L:K]
\]
for its generic degree.  The \(2\) in \(JC(2)\) denotes ambient dimension;
the separate condition \(d_F=2\) concerns the generic fiber.  The
nonvanishing argument below only uses a finite separable extension of
degree two.  The Keller condition enters through the nonzero constant
Jacobian and, at the end, through Galois rigidity.

\section{The explicit planar secant projector}
\label{sec:secant-projector}

The matrix below is the ordered multivariate B\'ezoutian matrix associated
to \(F\); compare \cite{McKean2023Bezoutians,SchejaStorch1975}.  We retain
the term \emph{secant matrix} because its determinant separates the
diagonal collision sheet from its complement.

Let
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C},
  \qquad
  \det JF=c\in\C^\times.
\]
Set
\[
  S=\C[x_1,x_2,y_1,y_2],\qquad
  \mathbf d=
  \begin{pmatrix}x_1-y_1\\x_2-y_2\end{pmatrix},\qquad
  \mathbf r=
  \begin{pmatrix}P(x)-P(y)\\Q(x)-Q(y)\end{pmatrix}.
\]

\subsection{Ordered divided differences}

For \(H\in\C[x_1,x_2]\), define
\[
  D_1H
  =
  \frac{H(x_1,x_2)-H(y_1,x_2)}{x_1-y_1},
\]
\[
  D_2H
  =
  \frac{H(y_1,x_2)-H(y_1,y_2)}{x_2-y_2}.
\]
Both quotients lie in \(S\).  The ordered telescoping identity is
\[
  H(x)-H(y)
  =D_1H\,(x_1-y_1)+D_2H\,(x_2-y_2).
\]
Define
\[
  M_F=
  \begin{pmatrix}
    D_1P&D_2P\\
    D_1Q&D_2Q
  \end{pmatrix}.
\]
Then
\[
  \mathbf r=M_F\mathbf d,\qquad
  M_F\big|_\Delta=JF.
\]
Let
\[
  \delta_F:=\det(M_F)\in S.
\]

\begin{proposition}[Secant annihilation]
\label{prop:secant-annihilation}
The secant determinant satisfies
\[
  \delta_FI_\Delta\subseteq I_R(F),
  \qquad
  \delta_F\big|_\Delta=c.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Multiplying \(\mathbf r=M_F\mathbf d\) by the adjugate matrix gives
\[
  \delta_F\mathbf d=\operatorname{adj}(M_F)\mathbf r.
\]
Both entries on the right belong to \(I_R(F)\).  Since the entries of
\(\mathbf d\) generate \(I_\Delta\), the first assertion follows.
Restriction to the diagonal gives
\[
  \delta_F\big|_\Delta
  =\det(M_F|_\Delta)
  =\det JF
  =c.
\]
\end{proof}

\subsection{The idempotent}

Let \(\bar\delta_F:=q_F(\delta_F)\) be the class of \(\delta_F\) in
\(C_F\), and put
\[
  e_F:=c^{-1}\bar\delta_F,\qquad
  p_F:=1-e_F=1-c^{-1}\bar\delta_F.
\]
The element \(e_F\) is the diagonal projector, while \(p_F\) is the
off-diagonal collision projector.

\begin{theorem}[Planar secant projector]
\label{thm:planar-secant-projector}
With the notation above,
\[
  e_F^2=e_F,\qquad
  p_F^2=p_F,\qquad
  e_Fp_F=0,\qquad
  e_F+p_F=1.
\]
Moreover,
\[
  \Obs(F)=C_Fp_F,\qquad
  \operatorname{Ann}_{C_F}(\Obs(F))=C_Fe_F,
\]
and \(p_F\) is the unique idempotent \(u\in C_F\) satisfying
\(\Obs(F)=C_Fu\).  Hence
\[
  C_F\simeq A\times C_Fp_F.
\]
In particular,
\[
  p_F=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Obs(F)=0.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Proposition~\ref{prop:secant-annihilation} gives
\[
  \bar\delta_F\Obs(F)=0,\qquad
  \bar\delta_F-c\in\Obs(F).
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  e_F\Obs(F)=0,\qquad e_F-1\in\Obs(F).
\]
Hence \(e_F(e_F-1)=0\), so \(e_F^2=e_F\); the corresponding identities
for \(p_F=1-e_F\) follow.

The second relation says \(p_F\in\Obs(F)\).  If
\(j\in\Obs(F)\), then
\[
  j=(e_F+p_F)j=p_Fj,
\]
so \(\Obs(F)=C_Fp_F\).  An element annihilates \(C_Fp_F\) exactly when its
\(p_F\)-part is zero, giving
\[
  \operatorname{Ann}_{C_F}(\Obs(F))=C_Fe_F.
\]

For uniqueness, let \(u^2=u\) and suppose \(C_Fu=C_Fp_F\).  Since
\(u\in C_Fp_F\), one has \(up_F=u\); since \(p_F\in C_Fu\), one has
\(up_F=p_F\).  Thus \(u=p_F\).

Complementary idempotents give
\[
  C_F\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }C_Fe_F\times C_Fp_F,\qquad
  a\longmapsto(ae_F,ap_F).
\]
Diagonal evaluation restricts to an isomorphism \(C_Fe_F\simeq A\):
if \(a=\bar\mu_F(r)\), then \(a=\bar\mu_F(re_F)\), while its kernel is
\(C_Fe_F\cap\Obs(F)=0\).  Thus \(C_F\simeq A\times C_Fp_F\).
Finally, \(\Obs(F)=C_Fp_F\) gives
\[
  p_F=0\Longleftrightarrow\Obs(F)=0.
\]
\end{proof}

Abstractly, the existence of an idempotent splitting is expected: a Keller
map is unramified, so its diagonal in the collision scheme is open, while a
morphism between affine schemes has closed diagonal
\cite{StacksUnramifiedDiagonalOpen,StacksAffineDiagonalClosed}.  The theorem
supplies the explicit B\'ezoutian representative and identifies the ideal it
generates.  The uniqueness just proved shows that the resulting \(p_F\) is
independent of the ordering used to write the divided differences.

\section{Conjugate-sheet fixed loci}
\label{sec:conjugate-cover}

Keep the planar Keller hypothesis, but impose no condition on \(d_F\).
Let \(N/K\) be the marked normal closure of \(L/K\), with inclusion
\(\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow N\), and put \(G=\Gal(N/K)\).  For
\(g\in G\), the conjugate source sheet is the \(\C\)-algebra map
\[
  \rho_g\colon A\longrightarrow N,\qquad a\longmapsto g(\iota(a)).
\]
All these maps agree on \(B\).  Define the all-conjugate algebra and its
invariant subalgebra by
\[
  E:=B[\rho_g(x_i):g\in G,\ i=1,2]\subseteq N,
  \qquad
  E^G:=\{e\in E:\sigma(e)=e\text{ for every }\sigma\in G\}.
\]
Thus \(B\subseteq E^G\subseteq E\), and the conjugate fields
\(g(\iota(L))\) generate \(\Frac(E)=N\).  The next result evaluates the
same projector as Theorem~\ref{thm:planar-secant-projector}, now on actual
collision pairs rather than solely in \(C_F\).

\begin{proposition}[Evaluated secant certificate]
\label{prop:evaluated-projector}
Let \(T\) be a complex integral domain and let
\(u,v\colon A\to T\) be distinct \(\C\)-algebra maps satisfying
\(u(P)=v(P)\) and \(u(Q)=v(Q)\).  They induce an evaluation
\(\operatorname{ev}_{u,v}\colon C_F\to T\).  Then
\[
  \operatorname{ev}_{u,v}(p_F)=1,
  \qquad
  \bigl(u(x_1)-v(x_1),\ u(x_2)-v(x_2)\bigr)=T.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Since \(u\ne v\), one coordinate difference is nonzero.  Evaluating
\(\delta_FI_\Delta\subseteq I_R(F)\) on the collision pair gives its
product with that difference equal to zero.  As \(T\) is a domain,
\(\operatorname{ev}_{u,v}(\bar\delta_F)=0\), and therefore
\(\operatorname{ev}_{u,v}(p_F)=1\).  But \(p_F\in\Obs(F)\), whose
evaluation lies in the ideal generated by the two coordinate
differences.  That ideal contains \(1\).
\end{proof}

For \(\sigma\in G\), write
\[
  J_\sigma:=\bigl(\sigma(e)-e:e\in E\bigr)\subseteq E.
\]
This ideal defines the scheme-theoretic fixed locus of \(\sigma\) on
\(\Spec E\).

\begin{theorem}[Fixed-locus certificate]
\label{thm:conjugate-fixed-locus}
If \(\sigma\in G\) is nonidentity, then \(J_\sigma=E\).  In particular,
\(\Spec(E/J_\sigma)=\varnothing\), and the inertia subgroup
\[
  \{\sigma\in G:\sigma(e)-e\in\mathfrak a\text{ for all }e\in E\}
\]
is trivial for every proper ideal \(\mathfrak a\subset E\).
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
The action of \(G\) on \(E\) is faithful because \(\Frac(E)=N\).
Hence some conjugate sheet \(\rho_g\) differs from
\(\rho_{\sigma g}=\sigma\circ\rho_g\).  The two sheets agree on \(B\),
so Proposition~\ref{prop:evaluated-projector} makes their coordinate
differences generate \(E\).  Each such difference lies in
\(J_\sigma\), up to sign.  Thus \(J_\sigma=E\).  If the displayed
inertia subgroup contained a nonidentity \(\sigma\), then
\(J_\sigma\subseteq\mathfrak a\), contradicting properness.
\end{proof}

The assertion concerns scheme-theoretic fixed loci: a prime of \(E\)
may still be preserved setwise by a nonidentity automorphism, whose
action on its residue field is nontrivial.  Nor does the theorem describe
points on a boundary omitted from \(\Spec E\).

\begin{corollary}[Finite \(G\)-cover over the invariant algebra]
\label{cor:conjugate-cover}
The inclusion \(E^G\subseteq E\) is finite \'etale and
faithfully flat.  Its canonical action-graph map is an isomorphism
\[
  \Theta\colon E\otimes_{E^G}E\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }
    \prod_{\sigma\in G}E,
  \qquad
  \Theta(a\otimes b)_\sigma=a\,\sigma(b).
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
Each \(e\in E\) is integral over \(E^G\), by the polynomial
\(\prod_{\sigma\in G}(T-\sigma(e))\).  The displayed finite family of
conjugate coordinates generates \(E\) over \(B\subseteq E^G\), so
\(E\) is module-finite over \(E^G\).  The unit difference ideals of
Theorem~\ref{thm:conjugate-fixed-locus} separate the action graphs.
The finite-group cover argument gives \(\Theta\) and finite projectivity
over \(E^G\); compare the free-quotient criterion
\cite{StacksFiniteFreeQuotient}.  The Keller condition makes
each conjugate source sheet formally unramified over \(B\).  Since those
sheets generate \(E\), formal unramifiedness passes to \(E/B\), hence
to \(E/E^G\).  Finite projectivity now makes \(E/E^G\) \'etale.
Finally, integrality makes \(\Spec E\to\Spec E^G\) surjective, so the
flat extension is faithfully flat.
\end{proof}

The base in Corollary~\ref{cor:conjugate-cover} is \(E^G\), not the
polynomial target ring \(B\).  In particular, neither finiteness over
\(B\) nor \(E^G=B\) follows from this cover.  The certificate does not
by itself prove \(\Obs(F)=0\) or \(JC(2)\).

\section{Quadratic generic collisions}
\label{sec:quadratic-collision}

We now work at the generic point.  The essential field-theoretic fact is
the degree-two marked-root decomposition.

\subsection{The quadratic self-tensor product}

Let \(L/K\) be finite separable of degree two.  Paper~I's marked-root
tensor decomposition applies to a primitive generator \(\alpha\): it
splits the marked root from the residual polynomial
\[
  m_\alpha(T)=(T-\alpha)h_\alpha(T)
  \qquad\text{in }L[T].
\]
Here \(h_\alpha\) has degree one, so its quotient algebra is another copy
of \(L\).  Thus Paper~I's general marked-root decomposition
\cite[Theorem~2.6]{VanDerVlugt2026CubicCollision} specializes as follows.

\begin{proposition}[Quadratic marked-root decomposition]
\label{prop:quadratic-tensor-product}
There is an \(L\)-algebra isomorphism
\[
  \Psi_\alpha\colon
  L\otimes_KL\xrightarrow{\ \sim\ }L\times L
\]
under which tensor multiplication
\[
  \mu_L\colon L\otimes_KL\longrightarrow L
\]
is first projection.  Consequently,
\[
  \Psi_\alpha\bigl(\ker(\mu_L)\bigr)=0\times L\neq0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Nonvanishing of the affine obstruction}

Combining the quoted generic collision bridge with
Proposition~\ref{prop:quadratic-tensor-product} transports the nonzero
second factor back to the affine obstruction.

\begin{theorem}[Quadratic collision nonvanishing]
\label{thm:quadratic-obstruction-nonzero}
Let \(F\colon\A^n_{\C}\to\A^n_{\C}\) be a polynomial map whose induced
extension \(L/K\) is finite separable of degree two.  Then
\[
  \Obs(F)\neq0.
\]
Equivalently,
\[
  I_R(F)\subsetneq I_\Delta.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Flatness of \(B\to K\) gives a canonical identification
\[
  K\otimes_B\Obs(F)
  \simeq
  \ker(1\otimes\bar\mu_F).
\]
Under the compatible quadratic product, the first-coordinate map has
kernel \(0\times L\), which is nonzero.  Therefore \(K\otimes_B\Obs(F)\neq0\), and hence
\(\Obs(F)\neq0\).  Since \(\Obs(F)=I_\Delta/I_R(F)\), this is equivalent
to strict containment.
\end{proof}

This theorem is independent of the Keller condition: generic degree two
already forces a residual generic collision.  For a planar Keller map, the
secant projector makes that factor explicit.

\begin{corollary}[Nonzero planar projector in generic degree two]
\label{cor:quadratic-projector-nonzero}
Let
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C},
  \qquad
  \det JF=c\in\C^\times.
\]
If \(d_F=2\), then
\[
  p_F\neq0.
\]
\end{corollary}

\begin{proof}
The Keller condition makes \(L/K\) finite separable.
Theorem~\ref{thm:quadratic-obstruction-nonzero} gives
\(\Obs(F)\neq0\), and
Theorem~\ref{thm:planar-secant-projector} gives
\(\Obs(F)=C_Fp_F\).  Therefore \(p_F\neq0\).
\end{proof}

\section{Galois rigidity and the collision contradiction}
\label{sec:rigidity-contradiction}

We now use the classical Galois case of the Jacobian conjecture.

\begin{theorem}[Classical Keller--Galois rigidity]
\label{thm:keller-galois-rigidity}
Let
\[
  F\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C}
\]
be a Keller map.  If the induced finite separable extension \(L/K\) is
normal, equivalently Galois, then \(F\) is a polynomial automorphism.
\end{theorem}

This is the classical normal/Galois case
\cite{Campbell1973,Razar1979,Wright1981}; we use it as a literature theorem
and do not reprove it here.

Every separable extension of degree two is normal: if the minimal polynomial
of a primitive generator has one root in \(L\), its other root also lies in
\(L\).  Thus Theorem~\ref{thm:keller-galois-rigidity} applies automatically
on the generic-degree-two Keller locus.

\begin{proposition}[Rigidity forces collision vanishing]
\label{prop:rigidity-projector-zero}
Let
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C},
  \qquad
  \det JF=c\in\C^\times.
\]
If \(d_F=2\), then Keller--Galois rigidity gives
\[
  \Obs(F)=0,\qquad p_F=0.
\]
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
The extension \(L/K\) is finite separable of degree two, hence Galois.
Theorem~\ref{thm:keller-galois-rigidity} makes \(F\) a polynomial
automorphism.  Theorem~\ref{thm:quoted-diagonal-kernel} then gives
\[
  I_R(F)=I_\Delta,\qquad \Obs(F)=0.
\]
Finally, Theorem~\ref{thm:planar-secant-projector} gives \(p_F=0\).
\end{proof}

\begin{theorem}[Exclusion of generic degree two]
\label{thm:no-planar-keller-degree-two}
No planar Keller map has generic degree two.  Equivalently, every
polynomial map
\[
  F=(P,Q)\colon\A^2_{\C}\longrightarrow\A^2_{\C},
  \qquad
  \det JF\in\C^\times,
\]
satisfies
\[
  d_F\neq2.
\]
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
Suppose that \(d_F=2\).
Corollary~\ref{cor:quadratic-projector-nonzero}, which reads the residual
factor in
\[
  K\otimes_BC_F\simeq_K L\times L,
\]
gives \(p_F\neq0\).  Proposition~\ref{prop:rigidity-projector-zero} gives
\(p_F=0\).  This is a contradiction.
\end{proof}

Equivalently, the two halves of the proof say
\[
  d_F=2\Longrightarrow\Obs(F)\neq0
\]
by the quadratic generic collision product, while
\[
  \text{\(F\) Keller and \(L/K\) normal}
  \Longrightarrow\Obs(F)=0
\]
by Galois rigidity and automorphism detection.  The secant construction
expresses both statements as nonvanishing and vanishing of one canonical
idempotent.

\section{Vanishing and the marked normal closure}
\label{sec:vanishing-criterion}

The same marked normal closure as in Paper~I characterizes when the
planar projector vanishes.  Throughout this section, \(F=(P,Q)\) is a
planar Keller map, with no generic-degree restriction.

Let \(N\) be the normal closure of \(L/K\), fix the embedding
\(\iota\colon L\hookrightarrow N\), and put
\[
  G=\Gal(N/K),\qquad H=\Gal(N/\iota(L)).
\]
These are the marked objects of Paper~I.

\begin{proposition}[Normal-closure criterion]
\label{prop:normal-closure-criterion}
For a planar Keller map, the following conditions are equivalent:
\[
  \begin{aligned}
  p_F=0
  &\Longleftrightarrow\Obs(F)=0
   \Longleftrightarrow L/K\text{ is normal}\\
  &\Longleftrightarrow N=\iota(L)
   \Longleftrightarrow H=1.
  \end{aligned}
\]
Equivalently, every conjugate source coordinate satisfies
\(g(\iota(x_i))\in\iota(L)\) for \(g\in G\) and \(i=1,2\).
\end{proposition}

\begin{proof}
Theorem~\ref{thm:planar-secant-projector} identifies \(p_F=0\) with
\(\Obs(F)=0\).  If \(\Obs(F)=0\), then \(I_R(F)=I_\Delta\).
Evaluating at the collision pair \((\iota(x),g(\iota(x)))\) gives
\(g(\iota(x_i))=\iota(x_i)\) for every \(g\in G\) and \(i=1,2\).
The conjugate source coordinates generate \(N\) over \(K\), so
\(N=\iota(L)\).  This equality is equivalent to normality of \(L/K\)
and, by Galois correspondence, to \(H=1\).  Conversely, normality gives
automorphy by Theorem~\ref{thm:keller-galois-rigidity}, hence
\(\Obs(F)=0\).  The same generation statement shows that containment
of all conjugate source coordinates in \(\iota(L)\) suffices.
\end{proof}

Normality of \(N/K\) is supplied by the construction; normality of
\(L/K\) is the criterion.  In the cubic nonnormal case of Paper~I,
\(\iota(L)\subsetneq N\) and \(H\ne1\).  Here the complementary
condition \(N=\iota(L)\), together with Keller rigidity, forces
\(L=K\).  In generic degree two, normality is automatic, so this
criterion gives the vanishing half of the contradiction in
Theorem~\ref{thm:no-planar-keller-degree-two}.  It does not assert
vanishing for every planar Keller map.

\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

For a planar Keller map, the divided-difference determinant gives
\[
  C_F\simeq A\times C_Fp_F,\qquad
  \Obs(F)=C_Fp_F,
\]
where \(p_F\) is the explicit off-diagonal collision projector.  On
distinct conjugate sheets its evaluation is \(1\).  Consequently every
nonidentity \(\sigma\in G\) has \(J_\sigma=E\), and the same
all-conjugate algebra satisfies
\[
  E\otimes_{E^G}E\simeq\prod_{\sigma\in G}E
\]
as a finite \'etale \(G\)-cover over \(E^G\).  These are
degree-independent, scheme-theoretic consequences of the projector.

Projector vanishing has the separate marked-closure description
\[
  p_F=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Obs(F)=0
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad N=\iota(L)
  \quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad H=1.
\]
This is the normal-branch counterpart to the nonnormal cubic construction
of Paper~I.

In generic degree two, the residual factor forces \(p_F\neq0\), while
normality and Keller--Galois rigidity force \(p_F=0\).  The incompatibility
excludes that degree.  Remaining possible counterexamples to \(JC(2)\)
have nonnormal function-field extensions of generic degree at least three.
The finite cover is over \(E^G\), not \(B\); it neither proves
\(E^G=B\) nor removes a possible boundary over \(\Spec B\).
This is why the fixed-locus certificate is not a proof of full
planar obstruction vanishing.
